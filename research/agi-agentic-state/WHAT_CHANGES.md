# What Changes With the Public Interest Framing

*Practical implications for the Agentic State Initiative*

---

## The Short Answer

**Not much changes in what you do. A lot changes in how you talk about it.**

The work is already aligned with the public interest framing — but the messaging leads with the mechanism (government capacity) rather than the mission (public interest). Flipping this makes the case stronger and the theory of change clearer.

---

## 1. Messaging Lead

### Currently
> "Governments should adopt agentic AI for public functions"

The subject is governments. The verb is adopt. AI is the object.

### With Public Interest Framing
> "In the AGI era, will the public interest be served?"

The subject is public interest. Governments become *one answer* to the question, not the question itself.

**Why this matters:** Funders care about impact, not institutional capacity for its own sake. Leading with the question makes the stakes clear; the government capacity work becomes the compelling answer.

---

## 2. Case Study Reframes

### Procurement Monitoring

| Current Frame | Public Interest Frame |
|---------------|----------------------|
| "AI agents can perform oversight functions" | "Public interest is currently underserved — corruption persists because oversight is episodic and under-resourced" |
| "We analyzed 2.2M records" | "We found patterns human auditors miss: EBC INC, Hungarian oligarchs, UK direct awards" |
| "Demonstrates government AI capability" | "Demonstrates AI can advance accountability — with or without government cooperation" |

The public interest frame makes the *problem* vivid (corruption, waste, capture) and positions the work as *solving* that problem, not just demonstrating technical capability.

### Apsakaleidja

| Current Frame | Public Interest Frame |
|---------------|----------------------|
| "AI helps legislators find errors" | "Better legislation serves citizens; errors harm them" |
| "PM endorsed it" | "Public interest validated it" |
| "Demonstrates legislative AI" | "Demonstrates AI advancing democratic quality" |

### The Substack Article

The "for government vs against government" framing is actually a false dichotomy in the public interest frame:

| Article Frame | Public Interest Frame |
|---------------|----------------------|
| "Agents pointed at government vs for government" | "Does this agent advance public interest?" |
| "Upside vs risk" | "Who benefits — narrow interests or the public?" |

Sometimes advancing public interest means helping government. Sometimes it means holding government accountable. Sometimes it means doing what government won't. These aren't opposites — they're all public interest work.

## 3. Theory of Change Expands

### Current Theory
```
Build government capacity 
    → Governments adopt agentic AI 
    → Better public services
```

Linear. Government-centric. Depends on government action.

### Public Interest Theory
```
Advance public interest through multiple vectors:
    
    1. Build government capacity (inside track)
       - Train civil servants
       - Deploy tools
       - Shape procurement
       
    2. Demonstrate public interest AI in action (permissionless track)
       - Procurement monitoring without government permission
       - Apsakaleidja without government commission
       - Prove what's possible
       
    3. Pressure/shame governments that don't act (accountability track)
       - Publish findings
       - Media engagement
       - Intervention packages (EBC INC, etc.)
       
    4. Enable civil society oversight (ecosystem track)
       - Tools for journalists
       - Tools for accountability orgs
       - Tools for citizens
```

**Key insight:** You're already doing all four vectors. The procurement monitoring is permissionless (vector 2). The intervention packages are accountability (vector 3). The vision paper is capacity building (vector 1). The framing just hasn't caught up with the practice.

## 4. The Inside-Out Gap (From Your Article)

You identified that the 12 layers describe what government should build, not what government faces from outside.

Public interest framing adds the outside-in question:

| Inside-Out (Current) | Outside-In (Addition) |
|---------------------|----------------------|
| What should government build? | What does the public need? |
| How should government adopt AI? | How should AI serve the public? |
| Government as subject | Public as subject |

These aren't the same question. Sometimes the public needs things government won't provide. Sometimes the public needs oversight *of* government. Sometimes the public needs services government is too slow to build.

**Implication for the 12 layers:** Consider adding a "Layer 0" or framing section that starts from public need, not government capability. Then the 12 layers become *how* to meet that need, not *what* to build in the abstract.

## 5. Who You Work With

### Current Scope
Primarily governments. Foundation funders who care about government capacity. Policy audience.

### Expanded Scope
- **Governments** — still primary, but framed as vehicles for public interest
- **Civil society** — accountability orgs, transparency groups
- **Journalists** — who use the tools for investigation
- **Citizens** — who benefit from oversight
- **International orgs** — who set norms
- **Other public interest actors** — foundations, unions, community orgs

The "lighthouse governments" framing still works, but it's lighthouse for *public interest*, not lighthouse for *government AI adoption*.

---

## 6. Speed Framing

### Currently
Speed matters because government needs to catch up to private sector AI capability.

### With Public Interest Framing
Speed matters because the window for shaping AGI outcomes is closing. If public interest actors aren't meaningful participants during AGI development, the resulting ecosystem will reflect whoever *was* at the table.

This is the "delta minimization" thesis from the earlier research: minimize the gap between frontier AI capability and public interest representation. Speed is the meta-variable because capabilities are advancing faster than institutions.

---

## 7. Concrete Changes

### Pitch Deck
- **Slide 1:** Lead with "In the AGI era, will the public interest be served?" not "Governments need agentic AI"
- **Problem statement:** Frame around public interest gap, not government capability gap
- **Case studies:** Emphasize corruption found, accountability advanced — not "we analyzed X records"

### Vision Paper (12 Layers)
- Add framing section: Start from public need, derive government role
- Address outside-in pressures explicitly (you noted this gap in the article)
- Consider "public interest test" for each layer: Does this serve the public or just government efficiency?

### Foundation Conversations
- McGovern ("AI for public purpose") — this framing is *exactly* what they fund
- Humanity AI Coalition ("democracy") — public interest includes democratic self-determination
- Rockefeller — "AI readiness" becomes "public interest readiness"

### Case Studies
- Lead with the problem (corruption, legislative errors, accountability gaps)
- Position AI as the solution to a public problem, not a demonstration of technical capability
- Emphasize permissionless innovation: "We didn't wait for government permission to advance public interest"

### Language
- "Public interest" > "public good" (less vague)
- "Accountable institutions" > "governments" (scoped to legitimate actors)
- "Meaningful participation" > "leadership" (more honest about ecosystem dynamics)

---

## 8. What Doesn't Change

- **The work itself** — procurement monitoring, Apsakaleidja, capacity building all continue
- **The urgency** — if anything, the public interest framing makes urgency clearer
- **The lighthouse strategy** — willing + able governments as proof points
- **The 12 layers** — still valid as operationalization framework
- **The technical approach** — agentic AI, agents that act not just advise

---

## Summary

| Dimension | Current | With Public Interest Framing |
|-----------|---------|------------------------------|
| Core question | How do we build government AI capacity? | How do we ensure public interest in AGI era? |
| Subject | Governments | The public |
| Theory of change | Linear (build capacity → adoption → outcomes) | Multi-vector (capacity + permissionless + accountability + ecosystem) |
| Who we work with | Primarily governments | Public interest actors broadly |
| Case study framing | Technical capability demonstration | Public problem solved |
| Speed rationale | Government catching up | Window for shaping AGI closing |
| Success metric | Government adoption | Public interest advanced |

**The bottom line:** The reframe makes the work more fundable, more compelling, and more honest about what you're actually doing. You're not just building government capacity — you're advancing public interest through multiple paths, of which government capacity is one (important) vector.
