# The Public Interest Frame: Recentering the Argument

*Developed 2026-02-15 after Luukas's correction*

---

## The Reframe

### What I Was Doing (Wrong)
I was treating the core question as: **"How do we build state capacity to govern AGI?"**

This is instrumental. It's about states. It's about capability-building. It centers AI as the subject.

### What the Core Question Actually Is
**"In an era of many AGIs, how do we ensure they — and what emerges from them — serve the public interest?"**

This is fundamentally different:
- **Subject**: Public interest, not AI
- **Context**: AGI era as backdrop, not focus
- **Assumption**: Multiple AGIs, not one singleton
- **Stance**: Proactive shaping, not reactive governing

---

## Developing the Frame Further

### 1. This Is Not (Primarily) an AI Question

**Usual AI alignment asks:** How do we make this system do what its operator wants, safely?
- Technical problem
- Focuses on the system
- Success = AI does what we intend

**Usual AI governance asks:** How do we regulate AI development and deployment?
- Policy problem
- Focuses on controlling actors
- Success = harmful uses prevented

**This asks:** How do we ensure the public interest is served in a world transformed by AGI?
- Political economy problem
- Focuses on outcomes for people
- Success = public good advanced

The distinction: **AI is the context, not the subject.** The subject is human flourishing, collective welfare, democratic self-determination — the things we mean when we say "public interest."

### 2. The Many-AGIs Assumption

We're not hypothesizing one AGI that either works or doesn't. We're hypothesizing:
- Multiple AGI-level systems
- Built by different actors (labs, governments, corporations, militaries)
- With different goals, values, and constraints
- Interacting with each other and with human systems

This changes the question from "Will THE AGI be aligned?" to "Will the AGI ecosystem serve public interest?"

**Implications:**
- No single point of control or failure
- Ecosystem dynamics matter (competition, cooperation, conflict)
- Governance is about shaping the ecosystem, not controlling one system
- Public interest must be embedded in *many* places, not just one

### 3. What "Public Interest" Means Here

"Public interest" is:
- **Contested**: Different people want different things
- **Plural**: Not a single objective to maximize
- **Political**: Determined through politics, not optimization
- **Dynamic**: Changes over time as society evolves

This is why states (as arenas for determining public interest) matter — but it's not *about* states. States are *instruments* for public interest, not ends in themselves.

**Key move:** We're not asking "How do we make governments better at AI?" We're asking "How do we make sure AGI serves the public?" States are one answer, but the question comes first.

### 4. The Counterfactual

Without intervention, AGI systems will reflect the interests of:
- Labs (commercial, research, prestige)
- Corporations (profit, competitive advantage)
- Militaries (national security, power projection)
- Individual operators (their specific goals)

These interests aren't necessarily *bad* — but they're not the *public* interest. They're private interests, institutional interests, national interests.

**The gap:** Between "what AGI builders/deployers want" and "what would serve humanity broadly."

**The work:** Ensuring *additional* representation for public interest beyond what emerges naturally from the interests of AGI developers and deployers.

### 5. Closer to Political Economy Than AI Safety

Classic political economy questions, applied to AGI:
- **Who decides** what AGI systems optimize for?
- **How are tradeoffs made** between competing interests?
- **Who bears costs** and who captures benefits?
- **What mechanisms** translate public preferences into outcomes?
- **What accountability** exists for AGI impacts?

These are questions about power, distribution, governance, legitimacy — not primarily about technical alignment.

**The insight:** Technical alignment (making AI do what operators intend) is necessary but not sufficient for public interest. You can have a perfectly aligned AGI serving perfectly narrow interests.

## Linking Back to the Agentic State Initiative

Now: how does this reframe connect to the work we've been doing?

### The Original Agentic State Thesis
From the Vision Paper and subsequent discussions:
> Governments should proactively adopt agentic AI for public functions, building capacity now rather than waiting until it's too late.

**Key elements:**
- Agentic AI (agents that act, not just advise)
- Public functions (procurement, legislation, service delivery)
- Government leadership (proactive, not reactive)
- 12 functional layers, L0-L5 progression

### How the Reframe Connects

| Original Framing | Public Interest Framing |
|-----------------|------------------------|
| "Governments should adopt agentic AI" | "Public functions should serve public interest in AGI era" |
| "Build state capacity" | "Ensure public interest is represented" |
| "Governments at the frontier" | "Public-interest actors competitive at the frontier" |
| "AI governance" | "Governance of AI-mediated outcomes" |

**The connection:** The Agentic State work is *one operationalization* of the public interest thesis.

The logic chain:
1. AGI will reshape everything, including public functions
2. Public functions should serve public interest (definitionally)
3. If public-interest actors (governments, but also others) aren't capable participants, AGI-mediated public functions will serve other interests
4. Therefore: build public-interest capability now

### The Case Studies as Evidence

**Public Function Monitoring (2026-02-08):**
- 2.2 million records analyzed
- Corruption patterns found (EBC INC, Hungarian oligarchs, UK direct awards)
- Live intervention opportunities identified

**What this demonstrates (in old framing):** AI agents can perform public oversight functions.

**What this demonstrates (in public interest framing):** 
- Public interest is currently underserved (corruption continues)
- AI capability can advance public interest (finding patterns humans miss)
- Permissionless innovation can fill gaps government doesn't

**Apsakaleidja v2 (2026-02-09):**
- Legislative error detection for Estonian Parliament
- PM Kallas endorsed v1

**What this demonstrates (in old framing):** AI can help legislators.

**What this demonstrates (in public interest framing):**
- Better legislation serves public interest
- AI capability in public-interest hands advances that
- The question isn't "can AI do this?" but "will it serve public interest?"

### The Foundation Pitch (2026-01-31)

Key differentiator identified:
> "Others govern AI that advises. We govern AI that acts."

**In public interest framing, this becomes:**
> "Others ask how to control AI. We ask how AI can serve the public."

The "agentic" focus is about *agency in the world* — systems that act, not just recommend. This is exactly where public interest stakes are highest, because acting systems shape outcomes directly.

## Where the Frameworks Fit Together

### Complementary Elements

1. **State capacity as instrument**
   - Public interest framing: States are one vehicle for public interest
   - Agentic State framing: Build state capability
   - Fit: Building state capability *serves* public interest (when states are accountable)

2. **"Many AGIs" assumption supports both**
   - Many AGIs = need for ecosystem governance
   - Ecosystem governance = public institutions matter
   - But also: many AGIs = multiple points of intervention

3. **The "delta minimization" thesis still holds**
   - In public interest framing: Minimize gap between AGI capability and public-interest representation
   - This is what the Agentic State does operationally

4. **Case studies as proof points**
   - Procurement monitoring shows public interest underserved
   - Apsakaleidja shows AI can advance public interest
   - Both support the thesis that capability matters

### Where They Potentially Clash

1. **"Government" vs "Public Interest"**
   - Agentic State focuses on governments
   - Public interest framing includes but transcends governments
   - Clash: Some governments don't serve public interest
   - Resolution: Scope to accountable institutions; use "public interest actors" not just "governments"

2. **Capability Building vs. Interest Representation**
   - Agentic State: Build technical capability
   - Public Interest: Ensure interests are represented
   - These aren't the same thing!
   - A highly capable government can still serve narrow interests
   - Resolution: Capability is necessary but not sufficient; need capability + accountability + representation

3. **"Leading" vs "Participating"**
   - Agentic State often frames governments as "leaders"
   - But in many-AGIs world, governments may be *participants* in an ecosystem they don't control
   - This is actually more honest: governments can shape, not command
   - Resolution: Reframe from "leadership" to "meaningful participation" — enough capability to have voice, not necessarily control

4. **Speed and Urgency**
   - Agentic State: "Build capacity now before it's too late"
   - Public Interest: Could be more patient if the goal is interest representation
   - Actually, no — the urgency holds because *who builds AGI* shapes *what it does*
   - If public-interest actors are absent during AGI development, outcomes are shaped by whoever is present

5. **National vs Global Public Interest**
   - Agentic State works with individual governments
   - But "public interest" is arguably global/humanity-wide
   - Working with Estonia or Canada serves those publics, not humanity
   - Resolution: This is actually fine — local public interest is legitimate, and the aggregate of many governments advancing their publics' interests is better than none doing so

## Synthesis: The Nested Framing

**Level 1 (Core):** How do we ensure public interest is served in an era of many AGIs?

**Level 2 (Theory):** Public-interest actors must be meaningful participants in AGI development and deployment; otherwise outcomes reflect only the interests of those who build and deploy.

**Level 3 (Mechanism):** States are uniquely important public-interest actors (enforcement, resources, legitimacy) — but not the only ones, and not automatically serving public interest.

**Level 4 (Operationalization):** The Agentic State Initiative builds accountable government capability to participate meaningfully in the AGI era, demonstrated through concrete public function improvements.

**The flow:**
- Core question (public interest) → 
- Theory (participation matters) → 
- Mechanism (states are key but not sufficient) → 
- Operationalization (build government capability + accountability)

This keeps the core question centered on **public interest**, not on states or AI per se.

## Implications for Messaging

### What to Lead With

**Old:** "Governments need to adopt agentic AI"
**New:** "In the AGI era, will the public interest be served?"

**Old:** "Build state capacity"
**New:** "Ensure public-interest actors can participate meaningfully"

**Old:** "AI governance"
**New:** "Governance for human flourishing in an AGI world"

### The Pitch Pivot

The Agentic State Initiative isn't about making governments better at AI.
It's about making sure the public interest is represented in a world being reshaped by AGI.

Governments are the focus *because* they're the primary vehicles for public interest in democratic societies — but the goal is the public, not the government.

## Open Questions After This Reframe

1. **What exactly is "public interest"?** 
   - Need to operationalize without collapsing into either vague platitudes or narrow metrics
   
2. **Non-state public-interest actors?**
   - Foundations, NGOs, unions, communities
   - How do they fit? Are they part of the strategy?

3. **Global vs. national public interest?**
   - Working with Estonia serves Estonians
   - Is there a "humanity-wide public interest" and how does this serve it?

4. **Accountability mechanisms?**
   - Building government capability isn't enough if governments don't serve their publics
   - What's the theory of accountability?

5. **What if AGI undermines public interest determination?**
   - If AGI can manipulate politics, the arena for determining public interest breaks
   - This is a deeper threat than just "who deploys AGI"
