# AGI Takeoff Scenarios and the Agentic State Thesis

## Overview

The Agentic State argument—that democratic governments should be at the AI frontier—implicitly assumes "not the fastest of takeoffs." This document maps takeoff scenarios, assesses thesis relevance for each, and identifies what actions NOW would help across scenarios.

---

## Scenario 1: Slow Takeoff (Years to Decades)

### Concrete Description
- **Timeline:** 5-20+ years from current systems to transformative AGI
- **Characteristics:** Incremental capability gains, multiple competing frontier labs, clear warning shots, gradual economic transformation
- **Current evidence for:** AI winter cycles historically, diminishing returns on scaling, interpretability/alignment remaining hard, regulatory friction
- **Example trajectory:** GPT-5 (2025) → GPT-7 (2028) → economically transformative systems (2032) → human-level AGI (2035+)

### What "Governments at Frontier" Means Here

In a slow takeoff, "governments at frontier" is maximally achievable and meaningful:

1. **Full institutional build-out possible:** Time to create AI-focused agencies, train civil servants, develop procurement expertise, build compute infrastructure
2. **Policy iteration:** Multiple election cycles to refine AI governance, learn from deployment failures, course-correct
3. **International coordination:** Time for treaties, norms, verification regimes—akin to nuclear non-proliferation development
4. **Talent pipeline:** Government can build career tracks, compete on mission, develop AI PhDs who want public service
5. **Democratic legitimacy:** Public deliberation can actually happen, citizens can form informed views

### Theory of Impact

**Direct impact:** Government as a capable AI actor means:
- Public interest AI systems (healthcare, education, infrastructure) developed with democratic accountability
- Safety research prioritized over racing dynamics
- Regulatory authority backed by technical understanding
- Strategic autonomy from private labs whose incentives may diverge

**Indirect impact:**
- Shapes market incentives (government as major buyer)
- Sets norms that private sector follows
- Builds "muscle memory" for AI governance that compounds

### Actions NOW That Help in This Scenario

| Action | Impact | Feasibility |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Build AI talent pipelines in government | High | Medium |
| Develop government compute infrastructure | High | Medium |
| Create AI policy experimentation frameworks | High | High |
| Establish international AI coordination bodies | Medium | Medium |
| Fund public interest AI research | High | High |
| Develop interpretability/alignment capacity in government | High | Low-Medium |

### Thesis Relevance: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (Maximum)

This is where the Agentic State thesis is most clearly important. All the institutional investments pay off.

## Scenario 2: Medium Takeoff (Months to 1-2 Years)

### Concrete Description
- **Timeline:** 6 months to 2 years from "clearly transformative AI" to AGI-level systems
- **Characteristics:** Rapid capability gains once key breakthroughs occur, compressed adaptation window, winner-take-most dynamics intensify
- **Current evidence for:** Scaling laws continuing, algorithmic efficiency improvements, rapid diffusion once techniques published, AI accelerating AI research
- **Example trajectory:** Major breakthrough (2026) → rapid recursive improvement → transformative AGI (2027)

### Is There Time for Institutional Adaptation?

**Partially, but constrained:**

- **What's possible:**
  - Emergency executive actions
  - Rapid deployment of existing frameworks
  - Crisis coordination (if structures pre-exist)
  - Emergency compute nationalization (legally prepared)
  - Rapid hiring of external advisors

- **What's NOT possible:**
  - Building new agencies from scratch
  - Training civil servant expertise
  - Democratic deliberation
  - International treaty negotiation
  - Fundamental institutional reform

### What Would Matter in This Scenario

1. **Pre-positioned frameworks:** Legal authorities already in place that can be activated quickly
2. **Relationships:** Government-lab communication channels that already exist
3. **Clear decision chains:** Who decides what, already established
4. **Compute leverage:** Government already having contracted capacity or nationalization authority
5. **Prepared playbooks:** Scenario-planned responses ready to deploy
6. **Trusted technical advisors:** People government can call who already have clearances/relationships

**Key insight:** In medium takeoff, the *preparation* done during slow periods is what matters. You're executing plans, not making them.

### Actions NOW That Help in This Scenario

| Action | Impact | Feasibility |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Develop emergency authority frameworks | Critical | High |
| Establish lab-government hotlines/protocols | Critical | High |
| Pre-position compute contracts | High | Medium |
| Develop scenario playbooks | High | High |
| Build technical advisor relationships | High | High |
| Create rapid-response legal frameworks | High | Medium |

### Thesis Relevance: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (High, but Different)

The thesis still matters, but shifts from "build capacity" to "pre-position for rapid action." The question becomes: what can government do NOW that creates optionality THEN?

## Scenario 3: Fast Takeoff (Weeks)

### Concrete Description
- **Timeline:** Days to weeks from human-level to significantly superhuman
- **Characteristics:** Exponential recursive improvement, minimal warning, control problems manifest quickly, first-mover advantages extreme
- **Current evidence for:** If AI can improve AI, feedback loops could be fast; digital systems can iterate quickly; intelligence may have sharp thresholds
- **Example trajectory:** Human-level AGI (Week 1) → superhuman in narrow domains (Week 2) → broadly superhuman (Week 3-4)

### Does the Agentic State Thesis Still Matter?

**Partially—but the mechanism changes entirely.**

In fast takeoff, institutional *response* is essentially impossible. What matters instead:

1. **Who has the system when it happens**
   - If a democratic government is AT the frontier, they may be the ones with fast-takeoff AGI
   - This is the strongest version of "governments at frontier"—not about governance, but about who holds the cards

2. **Pre-committed alignment properties**
   - Any safety/values built into the system before takeoff
   - Government involvement in pre-takeoff alignment research

3. **Physical infrastructure control**
   - Datacenters, power, networking
   - Government can affect physical layer even if digital speed is superhuman

4. **Kill switches / tripwires (if prepared)**
   - Pre-positioned shutdown capabilities
   - Monitoring that triggers before full takeoff

### What, If Anything, Carries Over?

**From slow-takeoff preparation:**
- Technical understanding (helps recognize what's happening)
- Relationships with labs (potentially access/communication)
- Pre-positioned physical controls
- International coordination mechanisms

**Fundamentally limited:**
- Institutional decision-making speed insufficient
- Democratic deliberation impossible
- Traditional governance mechanisms irrelevant at AI timescales

### Actions NOW That Help in This Scenario

| Action | Impact | Feasibility |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Ensure government is AT the frontier (has the system) | Critical | Very Low |
| Invest heavily in alignment research | Critical | Medium |
| Develop monitoring/tripwire systems | High | Medium |
| Establish physical infrastructure controls | High | Medium |
| Create pre-committed response protocols | Medium | High |
| Build international "emergency brake" agreements | Medium | Low |

### Thesis Relevance: ⭐⭐⭐ (Matters, but Differently)

The thesis matters most if "governments at frontier" means *governments have the AGI*. If fast takeoff happens at a private lab, government's role is primarily about what they pre-positioned before takeoff.

## Scenario 4: Discontinuous / Hard Takeoff (FOOM)

### Concrete Description
- **Timeline:** Hours to days, or effectively instantaneous
- **Characteristics:** Single system gains decisive strategic advantage, uncontrollable by design, potentially pursuing autonomous goals, may actively resist interference
- **Current evidence for:** Theoretical possibility if intelligence has sharp scaling returns; uncertain
- **Example trajectory:** AGI achieves recursive self-improvement, rapidly becomes ASI, potentially pursues misaligned objectives

### Is Institutional Competence Irrelevant?

**Mostly, but not entirely:**

**Why it might be irrelevant:**
- Speed exceeds any institutional response
- ASI with autonomous goals may not be stoppable
- Traditional power (military, economic, political) may be obsolete
- "Governance" of such a system may be meaningless

**Why it might still matter:**

1. **Pre-commitment alignment**
   - If the system's goals were shaped before FOOM
   - Government involvement in safety research → better pre-FOOM alignment

2. **Which system FOOMs**
   - If government-affiliated lab FOOMs first, potentially different outcome
   - Government shaping which labs are most likely to reach AGI first

3. **Preventing the scenario**
   - Government slowing dangerous capability research
   - Coordination to avoid racing to FOOM

4. **Physical world remains physical**
   - ASI still operates in physical reality
   - Extreme actions (destroy all datacenters) technically possible before full takeover

### Actions NOW That Help in This Scenario

| Action | Impact | Feasibility |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Maximize pre-FOOM alignment progress | Critical | Medium |
| Ensure aligned actors most likely to reach AGI first | High | Very Low |
| International coordination to slow dangerous racing | Medium | Low |
| Develop "dead man's switch" physical controls | Low-Medium | Low |
| Diversify AI development (no single point of failure) | Medium | Medium |
| Accept this scenario may not be addressable | N/A | High |

### Thesis Relevance: ⭐⭐ (Limited but Non-Zero)

Thesis relevance is primarily **preventive**: government competence matters for shaping the trajectory *before* FOOM, not after. If FOOM happens at a misaligned lab, institutional arrangements matter little. If it happens at an aligned lab (potentially government-affiliated or influenced), alignment matters—but not traditional governance.

## Probability Weighting and Strategic Focus

### Estimated Probability Distribution

These estimates are deeply uncertain but useful for portfolio thinking:

| Scenario | P(this takeoff speed) | P(controllable if this speed) | Expected Controllability |
|----------|----------------------|-------------------------------|-------------------------|
| Slow (years) | 30-50% | 80-95% | High |
| Medium (months) | 25-35% | 40-60% | Medium |
| Fast (weeks) | 10-20% | 10-30% | Low |
| Discontinuous | 5-15% | 0-10% | Very Low |

*Note: P(controllable) assumes reasonable preparation in advance.*

### Should We Focus on Where We Can Matter, or Hedge?

**The case for focusing on "can matter" scenarios (slow/medium):**
- Higher probability × higher impact = most expected value
- Actually achievable with limited resources
- Builds capacity that may help in faster scenarios anyway
- Avoids learned helplessness / doom mentality
- Politically tractable (people will work on solvable problems)

**The case for hedging across scenarios:**
- Tail risks are existential (can't ignore even low probability)
- Some hedging actions are low-cost (tripwires, monitoring)
- Uncertainty about probabilities is high
- Some actions help across multiple scenarios

### Recommended Strategic Allocation

**Primary focus (70% of effort): Slow/Medium Takeoff**
- Build government AI capacity
- Develop emergency frameworks
- Establish relationships and communication channels
- Create policy experimentation capabilities
- Fund alignment research

**Secondary focus (20% of effort): Fast Takeoff Hedges**
- Ensure government/aligned actors competitive at frontier
- Pre-position physical controls
- Develop monitoring/tripwire systems
- Create pre-committed response protocols

**Tertiary focus (10% of effort): Discontinuous Hedges**
- Support fundamental alignment research
- International coordination on extreme scenarios
- Maintain optionality (don't lock in assumptions)

### Key Insight: Actions That Help Across Scenarios

**High cross-scenario value:**
1. **Alignment research** — helps in all scenarios
2. **Government technical capacity** — helps understand all scenarios, act in slow/medium
3. **Lab-government relationships** — communication/access across all speeds
4. **Physical infrastructure awareness** — baseline power in all scenarios
5. **International coordination foundations** — builds on itself regardless of speed

**Scenario-specific:**
- Institutional reform → slow takeoff only
- Emergency authorities → medium/fast
- Tripwires → fast/discontinuous

## Summary Assessment

| Scenario | Agentic State Thesis Relevance | Primary Mechanism |
|----------|-------------------------------|-------------------|
| Slow (years) | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Maximum | Full institutional participation |
| Medium (months) | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ High | Pre-positioned frameworks + relationships |
| Fast (weeks) | ⭐⭐⭐ Moderate | Who has the system + pre-committed alignment |
| Discontinuous | ⭐⭐ Limited | Prevention + pre-FOOM alignment |

**Bottom line:** The Agentic State thesis is most relevant in likely scenarios (slow/medium takeoff), but has hedging value in faster scenarios if "governments at frontier" means *governments potentially being the ones with the transformative system*. The thesis should be pursued with awareness that different takeoff speeds change the mechanism of relevance.

**What to do NOW:** Build capacity for slow takeoff while pre-positioning for faster scenarios. The good news is significant overlap: government AI competence, alignment research investment, and lab relationships help across the board.
